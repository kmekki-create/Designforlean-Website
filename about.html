ABOUT

We think certification should mean you can actually do the work.

Design for Lean was built around one belief: a Lean Six Sigma belt should prove you improved something real — not that you memorized a glossary.

OUR APPROACH

Multiple-choice exams don't fix processes. People do.

Most certification programs optimize for a passing exam score. A Design for Lean Six Sigma certification optimizes for something harder to fake: a documented, mentor-reviewed improvement to a real process, using the same DMAIC method taught at every level.

That means slower cohorts, smaller mentor caseloads, and a curriculum that gets rewritten whenever a mentor tells us a module isn't translating into practice. We'd rather be the certification that's harder to get and worth more once you have it.

Some organizations don't have time to wait for a cohort to finish, though — which is why the same Black Belts who mentor our capstones also run hands-on consulting engagements when a process needs fixing this quarter, not this year.

WHAT WE OPTIMIZE FOR

Evidence over opinion

Every capstone includes before/after data, not just a narrative.

Mentorship over multiple choice

A certified Master Black Belt reads and challenges your actual work.

Momentum over perfection

Small, shippable improvements beat a perfect plan that never launches.

CURRICULUM STANDARD

Built on an open, recognized body of knowledge

Rather than inventing our own definition of what a "Green Belt" should know, we map every module to the American Society for Quality (ASQ) Lean Six Sigma Body of Knowledge — the same reference standard used across the industry. That means what you learn here transfers cleanly to any employer, and what we teach is auditable against a public standard, not a marketing claim.
